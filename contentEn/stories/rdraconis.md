# **R DRACONIS**
### *The Pulsating Giant*

### **Type:** Mira-type variable red giant (M4.5–M6)
### **Distance:** ~2,600 light-years
### **Visual magnitude:** Variable, approximately +6.9 to +13.0

Deep in the constellation Draco lives an aging star that seems to have a heartbeat of its own. Unlike our Sun, whose brightness stays nearly constant, R Draconis expands and contracts rhythmically every eight months. With each cosmic beat, its size and temperature shift dramatically, making it shine hundreds of times brighter at its peak than at its dimmest point.

R Draconis is an evolved red giant located in the constellation Draco. It belongs to the Mira class of variable stars, a group of stars that undergo substantial and recurring brightness changes due to pulsations in their outer layers.

As the star expands and contracts, its surface temperature, radius and energy output change. These processes produce significant variations in its luminosity, particularly in visible light. Its pulsation cycle has an approximate period of 246 days.

Infrared observations allow astronomers to study the radiation emitted by its atmosphere and analyze how its energy distribution changes across different wavelengths. This is particularly interesting in evolved stars, whose atmospheres may contain molecules and dust that affect the observed radiation.


### *Did you know?*
Mira variable stars can change their brightness so dramatically that some become visible to the naked eye at their brightest and practically disappear from view at their faintest. R Draconis can experience several orders of magnitude of variation in its observed brightness!
