# **RW AURIGAE**
### *The Unstable Young Star*

- **Type:** Young multiple stellar system; classical T Tauri star
- **Distance:** ~185 parsecs (≈603 light-years)
- **Visual magnitude:** Variable, approximately +9.2 to +13.6

Young stars are often restless, but few behave as dramatically as RW Aurigae. It shone steadily for decades until it suddenly began suffering mysterious blackouts lasting for months. Astronomers discovered that massive clouds of dust and debris from its own planetary disk pass right in front of it, blocking its light like a cosmic curtain.

RW Aurigae is a young stellar system located in the Taurus-Auriga star-forming region. Its components are still in the early stages of stellar evolution and are surrounded by circumstellar material, mainly composed of gas and dust.

One of its most interesting characteristics is its variability. Its brightness can change considerably due to several physical processes, including material falling onto the star (accretion) and dusty structures passing through our line of sight, partially blocking its light. These phenomena cause its appearance to change irregularly over time.

Infrared observations are particularly useful for studying systems like this because they allow astronomers to investigate the emission from dust and material surrounding young stars. SPHEREx can provide information about how the emitted energy is distributed across different wavelengths.


### *Did you know?*
RW Aurigae has experienced particularly deep dimming episodes, during which its brightness decreased significantly. Astronomers are still investigating the processes responsible for these changes!
