# **WISE 0855−0714**
### *The Cosmic Freezer*

- **Type:** Y2-type brown dwarf
- **Distance:** ~7.43 light-years
- **Visual magnitude:** Not detected in visible light; infrared W2 magnitude ≈ +13.89

When we think of a star, we usually picture a blazing sphere of fire and light. Yet in 2014, the WISE space telescope shattered that idea by finding an object that defied all expectations. It was a brown dwarf so cold that its surface temperature resembles Earth's North Pole. In fact, it is the first object outside our Solar System known to have clouds of water ice.

WISE 0855−0714 is one of the coldest known substellar objects. It is an extraordinarily faint brown dwarf discovered through infrared observations made by NASA's Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer (WISE). Although it is relatively close to Earth, it is extremely difficult to detect because of its low temperature and luminosity.

Its effective temperature is estimated to be approximately 225–260 kelvin (about −48 to −13 °C, or −55 to +8 °F). Under such cold conditions, its atmosphere may contain water clouds and other condensed substances, making it a fascinating object for studying the atmospheres of substellar bodies.

One of its most interesting characteristics is its high proper motion. Because of its proximity and spatial velocity, its apparent position changes considerably relative to background stars when observations taken on different dates are compared.


### *Did you know?*
WISE 0855−0714 is so cold that its estimated atmospheric temperature can approach that of a household freezer. Its temperatures are comparable to those found in some giant planets!
