# **Moving Pictures**
### *Beyond what the eye can tell.*

In 1930, Clyde Tombaugh found Pluto by “blinking” two photographs of the same patch of sky taken days apart. Anything that jumped between them was something close and moving. People still hunt for a hypothetical distant “Planet X” the same way.
Now you can do it too, with real, recent data from NASA’s SPHEREx space telescope, which maps the whole sky in infrared. The app lines up images of the same spot taken on different dates and plays them like a flipbook, so stars that move or change in brightness stand out.

Made for curious people, not specialists: we don’t just show you the sky, we teach you what you’re looking at.

## How it works
1.	Pick a spot. Type sky coordinates or choose one of five famous objects.
2.	We fetch the data. The app finds and downloads SPHEREx images of that spot from different dates.
3.	Discover the motion. Watch them play as an animation, and spot what moves, what flickers, and what stays still.
