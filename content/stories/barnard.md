# **BARNARD'S STAR**
### *The Fast-Moving Neighbor*

### **Type:** Red dwarf (M4V)
### **Distance:** ~5.96 light-years
### **Visual magnitude:** +9.51

For a long time, stars appeared fixed in the sky until astronomer Edward Emerson Barnard discovered in 1916 that Barnard's Star was noticeably shifting position. Its movement is detectable because the star is unusually close to Earth, making it one of the stars with the highest known proper motion.

Barnard's Star is a small and relatively cool red dwarf located in the constellation Ophiuchus. It is estimated to have approximately 16% of the Sun's mass and around 20% of its radius. Due to its low mass, it consumes its nuclear fuel much more slowly than larger stars. Its surface temperature is approximately 3,100–3,230 kelvin (5,120–5,354 °F), roughly half the Sun's surface temperature.

As a result of these characteristics, its luminosity is extremely low: it emits only a tiny fraction of the energy produced by our star. With an apparent visual magnitude of approximately +9.51, Barnard's Star is completely invisible to the naked eye. It also emits a significant proportion of its energy at infrared wavelengths.

Its most extraordinary feature is its exceptionally high proper motion. Because of its proximity to Earth and its movement through space, its apparent position changes rapidly relative to distant background stars. However, even this movement is subtle when observed over short periods.


### *Did you know?*
Barnard's Star moves approximately 10.4 arcseconds across our sky every year, making it one of the stars with the highest known proper motion.
